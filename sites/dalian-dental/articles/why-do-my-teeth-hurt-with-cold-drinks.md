## What is tooth sensitivity?

Under the hard outer enamel, and below the gum line under a covering of gum, teeth have a softer layer called dentine. When dentine is exposed, cold, hot, sweet or acidic things and even cool air can cause a brief, sharp pain. Researchers describe this as dentin hypersensitivity.

## Common causes

- Gum recession, where the gum pulls back and exposes the root surface. Reviews describe gum loss as a major predisposing factor.
- Worn enamel, for example from acidic food and drink or from wear over many years.
- Gum disease, which can also loosen the gum around the teeth.
- Other dental problems that feel similar, such as a cavity, a cracked tooth or a worn filling.

## Why a dentist should check

Several dental conditions can feel like simple sensitivity. Published reviews note that diagnosis is difficult for this reason, and that there is no single universally accepted guideline for telling them apart. An examination lets your dentist rule out decay or a cracked tooth before treating sensitivity.

## Treatment options

Reviews recommend starting with the most conservative options and moving to more invasive care only if needed.

- Desensitising toothpaste is described as the most effective and least invasive first step.
- Treating the cause, such as managing gum disease or protecting worn areas.
- Treatments applied by your dentist for stubborn areas.

## Prevention

Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth, and ask your dentist to check your brushing technique and your gums at your regular visits. Limiting frequent acidic drinks may also help protect enamel.

## When to see a dentist

Book a visit if the pain lasts after the cold is gone, wakes you at night, comes with swelling, or is in one tooth only. These signs can point to a problem that toothpaste will not fix.
