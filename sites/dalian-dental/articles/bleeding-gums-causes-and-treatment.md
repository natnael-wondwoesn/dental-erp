## What causes bleeding gums?

The main cause is plaque, a sticky film of bacteria that builds up on teeth when brushing and cleaning between the teeth is not thorough enough. Plaque can harden into tartar, which only a dental professional can remove. Bacteria in plaque and tartar inflame the gums, and inflamed gums bleed easily.

## Symptoms to look for

- Gums that are red, swollen or tender and bleed easily.
- Persistent bad breath.
- In more advanced disease, gums that pull away from the teeth so teeth look longer, teeth that feel loose or sensitive, and discomfort when chewing.

## Who is at higher risk?

Smoking or other tobacco use is the most significant risk factor and can also make treatment less successful. Other factors linked to gum disease include diabetes, older age, some medicines and conditions, and hormonal changes.

## Treatment options

Treatment starts with removing plaque and tartar through a professional cleaning and improving daily cleaning at home. More advanced disease needs further treatment from your dentist, and the sooner it is caught the simpler treatment is.

## Prevention

- Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.
- Clean between your teeth every day with floss or interdental brushes. A Cochrane review found that adding these to brushing may reduce gum inflammation and plaque more than brushing alone, though the certainty of the evidence is low, and interdental brushes may work better than floss.
- Have regular dental check-ups and cleanings.
- Stop using tobacco.

## When to see a dentist

See a dentist if bleeding continues for more than a week or two after you improve your cleaning, or if you notice loose teeth, receding gums, pus or constant bad breath.
