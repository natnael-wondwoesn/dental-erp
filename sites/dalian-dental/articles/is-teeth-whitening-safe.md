## How whitening works

Most whitening products use hydrogen peroxide, or carbamide peroxide, which breaks down into hydrogen peroxide. The peroxide enters the tooth and lightens the stains. Products differ in strength and in how long they stay on the teeth.

## What are the side effects?

Reviews of the research describe two common ones:

- Tooth sensitivity, usually temporary.
- Irritation of the gums.
  Other, rarer effects have been reported, particularly with strong products or prolonged use. Review authors recommend using low concentrations and avoiding prolonged, concentrated exposure.

## How to reduce the risk

- Have your teeth examined first. Untreated cavities, gum disease or worn enamel can make whitening uncomfortable.
- Follow the instructions exactly, and do not use products for longer than directed.
- Choose low-dose products and avoid prolonged, concentrated exposure, as review authors recommend.
- Ask your dentist about professional whitening, which is supervised.

## What whitening cannot do

Whitening does not change the colour of crowns, veneers or fillings, and it works best on natural teeth stained by food, drink or age. Deep staining or damaged teeth may need a different treatment, such as veneers.

## When to see a dentist

Stop and see your dentist if you have strong or lasting sensitivity, painful or bleeding gums, or white patches on the gums.
