## What is a dental implant?

An implant is a titanium post that is placed in the jawbone, where it holds a crown, bridge or denture in place. Implants are placed and restored by dentists and oral and maxillofacial surgeons.

## How well do implants last?

A systematic review of the 5-year survival of implant-supported single crowns found:

- About 96.8% of implants survived at 5 years.
- About 94.5% of the crowns on them survived at 5 years.
- Metal-ceramic crowns had higher survival than all-ceramic crowns in that review.

## What complications can happen?

The same review reports that complications were common even when survival was high:

- Inflammation of the gum and bone around the implant (peri-implantitis) or soft-tissue problems occurred beside about 9.7% of crowns over 5 years.
- Loosening of the screw or abutment occurred in about 12.7%.
- Fractures of the porcelain were about 4.5%, and fracture of the implant itself was very rare.
  This is why follow-up care matters. Problems found early are easier to correct.

## Caring for an implant

- Clean around the implant every day, as you would a natural tooth.
- Attend regular check-ups so that your dentist can check the gum, the bone and the crown.
- Tell your dentist about any bleeding, swelling or looseness straight away.
- Keep your gums healthy. Gum disease and smoking are covered in our article on bleeding gums.

## Alternatives

Bridges and dentures can also replace missing teeth. Your dentist can explain which option fits your mouth, health and budget.

## When to see a dentist

See your dentist if the crown feels loose, the gum around the implant is red, swollen or bleeds, or the implant is painful.
